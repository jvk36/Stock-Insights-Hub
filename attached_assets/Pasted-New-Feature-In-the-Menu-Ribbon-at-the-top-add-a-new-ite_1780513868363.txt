New Feature: In the Menu Ribbon at the top, add a new item ‘Macro Summary’ next to the ‘Stock Analysis’ item. It will have the following sub-tabs, each of which has several cards with indicators and such. Clicking on any indicator should show a chart/graph showing the value of that indicator over time. Details of each sub-tab follows: 1) Overview: Key macroeconomic readings at a glance. The tab will have three sections – a) Latest Key Readings At A Glance: A table with columns for Indicator, Value (color coded), Signal, and Status for the following – GDP Growth (Annualized), CPI (YoY), Unemployment Rate, Fed Funds Rate, 10-Year Treasury Yield, and Industrial Production (YoY). Clicking on a row should show a Chart showing the indicator value over time, b) Signal Dashboard: A set of four Informatory Cards showing the values, signal, and an explanation of what it means for the following – Yield Curve (2s10s), Credit Spreads (HY OAS), Labor Market (NFP MoM), and Recession Probability. Clicking on a card should show a Chart showing the signal value over time, and c) Market Cycle & Yield Curve: A set of two Cards showing the following – i) Market Cycle Phase with a Confidence Indicator and a visual selector among Recovery, Early Expansion, Mid Expansion, Late Expansion, Early Contraction, and Recession, and ii) US Treasury Yield Curve that shows a long-term chart of the 2s10s curve. Data Source: FRED (Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis), 2) GDP: GDP activity indicators. It will have a table with columns for Indicator, Latest Value (color coded), Source, Frequency, and Type for the following – Real GDP (Annualized), Atlanta Fed GDPNow, ISM Manufacturing PMI, ISM Services PMI, Industrial Production, Retail Sales (ex-auto), Durable Goods Orders, and Building Permits. Clicking on a row should show a Chart showing the indicator value over time. Data Source: BEA, Atlanta Fed, Conference Board, Federal Reserve, and Census Bureau. 3) Inflation: Consumer prices, producer prices, and market-based inflation expectations vs 2% Fed target. The tab will have three sections – a) Headline Readings: A set of four cards for CPI (YoY), Core CPI (YoY), PCE Deflator (YoY), and Core PCE (YoY) – the cards should show the value and visual cue in relation to the 2% target, and a text to say below, At or Above Target. Clicking on a row should show a Chart showing the indicator value over time, b) CPI Component Breakdown – YoY %: A Table with Columns for Component, YoY %, and Value/Status column indicating Below, Above, Well Above, etc. against Target for the following: Shelter/OER, Supercore (Services ex-Energy), Food at Home, Energy, and New Vehicles. Clicking on a row should show a Chart showing the indicator value over time, and c) Full Inflation Data Suite: A Table with Columns for Indicator, Latest Value (color coded), Source, and Why It Matters (Explanation) for the following: CPI (All Items), Core CPI (ex-Food & Energy), PCE Deflator, Core PCE, PPI (Final Demand), 5Y5Y Breakeven Rate, Michigan Inflation Expectation (1Y), Owners’ Equivalent Rent (OER), and Supercore CPI. Clicking on a row should show a Chart showing the indicator value over time. Data Source: BLS, BEA, Federal Reserve, University of Michigan, 4) Labor: Employment, wages, and workforce participation indicators. The tab will have two sections – a) Labor Market Health: A set of four Cards for Nonfarm Payrolls MoM, Unemployment Rate (U-3), Avg Hourly Earnings YoY, and Job Openings (JOLTS). Each should show the value, what it means (Healthy, Weak, Tight, etc.), and explanation of the ranges. Clicking on a card should show a Chart showing the indicator value over time, and b) Full Labor Data Suite: A Table with Columns for Indicator, Latest Value (color coded), Source, and Signal for the following: Nonfarm Payrolls (NFP), ADP Private Payrolls, U-3 Unemployment Rate, U-6 Unemployment Rate, Labor Force Participation Rate, Prime-Age LFPR (25-54), Job Openings (JOLTS), Quits Rate, Initial Jobless Claims, Continuing Claims, and Average Weekly Hours. Clicking on a row should show a Chart showing the indicator value over time. Data Source: BLS, ADP, DOL, and FRED, 5) Financial Conditions: Interest rates, credit spreads, volatility, and monetary policy indicators. The tab will have two sections – a) Fed & Interest Rates: A set of four cards for Fed Funds Rate, 2Y Treasury Yield, 10Y Treasury Yield, and 2s10s Yield Spread with the Rate, What it Means (Restrictive, Normal Flat, etc.), and an Explanation. Clicking on a card should show a Chart showing the indicator value over time., and b) Financial Conditions Indicators: A Table with Columns for Indicator, Latest Value (color-coded), Source, Signal (Elevated, Very Tight, Very Loose, Normal, etc.), and What to Watch (Explanation) for the following: Federal Funds Rate, 2Y-10Y Yield Spread, 30Y Mortgage Rate, HY Credit Spread (OAS), IG Credit Spread (OAS), Chicago Fed NFCI, Goldman FCI Index, VIX (Implied Volatility), TED Spread, USD Index (Broad), WTI Crude Oil, and M2 Money Supply (YoY). Clicking on a row should show a Chart showing the indicator value over time. Data Source: FRED, FOMC, ICE BofA, CBOE, Feddie Mac, EIA, and Chicago Fed, 6) Global: Central bank rates, inflation, and FX indicators. The tab will have one card - a) Key Global Indicators: A Table with Columns for Indicator, Latest Value (color-coded), Source, Signal (Neutral, Restrictive, etc.), and Impact (Explanation) for the following: ECB Deposit Rate, BoE SONIA Rate, Eurozone CPI (YoY), Japan CPI (YoY), UK CPI (YoY), China CPI (YoY), Eurozone Unemployment, Brent Crude ($/bbl), EUR/USD, USD/JPY, and USD/CNY. Clicking on a row should show a Chart showing the indicator value over time, Data Source: ECB, BoE, Eurostat, MIC Japan, ONS, NBS, Eurostat, EIA, Fed, and PBoC/Fed, and 7) DIY Investor Guide: It will have two sections – a) How To Read Each Indicator: A set of six cards for PMI (Purchasing Managers’ Index), Yield Curve (2s10s Spread), Nonfarm Payrolls (NFP), PCE vs. CPI – What is the Difference?, Credit Spreads, Leading vs. Lagging Indicators. Each Card should have the following details – The ranges and what they indicate, a para explaining what it is, Data Source, and Frequency of Data Release, and b) Data Release Calendar: a Table with Columns for Frequency (Weekly, Monthly, Quarterly, and As Needed), Key Releases – show ~25 of the most important ones (Initial Jobless Claims, JOLTS, GDP, etc.), and Release Date Details (Thursday, 1st Friday, etc.).
